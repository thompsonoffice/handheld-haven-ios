ENTRY: OQO model 01 and 01+
Maker: OQO, Inc., San Francisco (2000 to 2009)
Released: Model 01: retail shipments from October 14, 2004. Model 01+: September 27, 2005; Tablet PC Edition version January 4, 2006 (Wikipedia).
Launch price: Not documented in sources used.

WHAT IT CAN DO
Guinness World Records called the OQO the smallest full-powered, full-featured PC in 2005 (Wikipedia). A slide-out keyboard pocket computer running Windows XP.

HARDWARE
Processor: Transmeta Crusoe, 1 GHz.
Memory: 256 MB (01); 512 MB (01+).
Graphics: not documented
Storage: 20 GB hard drive (01); 30 GB (01+).
Display: 5-inch-class 800 by 480 transflective screen with a Wacom pen digitizer.
Battery: not documented
Weight and size: 0.9 pounds (410 g); 4.9 by 3.4 by 0.9 inches.
Operating system: Windows XP Home or Professional; Tablet PC Edition 2005 on the 01+.
Wi-Fi and network: 802.11b.
Bluetooth: Bluetooth.
Cellular: not documented
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): USB 1.1 (USB 2.0 on 01+), FireWire 400, headphone port.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): Built-in microphone; internal speaker added on 01+.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
Slide-out thumb keyboard; Wacom pen.

HOW IT FEELS
4.9 by 3.4 by 0.9 inches and under a pound: palm-sized and thick. The screen slides to reveal a thumb keyboard (Wikipedia describes the slide-out keyboard; detailed tactile reviews not found).

BLIND USABILITY
These 2004 to 2010 machines ran Windows XP, Vista or 7. Screen readers of that era included JAWS, Window-Eyes and early NVDA (NVDA's first releases date from 2006; current NVDA does not support XP). Narrator in XP and Vista was minimal. No source found documents blind use of these specific UMPCs. Their value today is historical; batteries, drives and drivers are aging. Small hardware keyboards, where present, can be learned by touch.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: very low by modern standards. Suitable only for very old PC games and simple audio games that still support Windows XP or 7 (check each game).

UNCERTAIN
Launch price not found.

ENTRY: OQO model 02, e2 and the unreleased model 2+
Maker: OQO, Inc.
Released: Model 02 introduced by Bill Gates at CES January 2007; e2 (Europe and Asia, HSDPA) September 17, 2007; model 2+ announced CES 2009 but never shipped; OQO ceased operations May 2009 (Wikipedia).
Launch price: Not documented. Laptop Mag (2007) said an OQO model 02 with integrated EV-DO cost about US$300 more than the US$999 Fujitsu U810.

WHAT IT CAN DO
Then the world's smallest fully functional Windows Vista PC.

HARDWARE
Processor: VIA C7-M ULV at 1.2, 1.5 or 1.6 GHz. Model 2+: Intel Atom 1.33 or 1.86 GHz.
Memory: 512 MB or 1 GB (2 GB on 2+).
Graphics: not documented
Storage: 40 to 120 GB hard drive, or 32/64 GB SSD.
Display: 5-inch 800 by 480 with active digitizer; 2+ would have been the first PC with an AMOLED screen.
Battery: not documented
Weight and size: 1 pound (450 g); about 413 g with SSD and standard battery.
Operating system: Windows XP Professional or Vista Business.
Wi-Fi and network: 802.11a/b/g.
Bluetooth: Bluetooth 2.0.
Cellular: Optional EV-DO (Verizon or Sprint) in the US; HSDPA in the e2.
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): USB 2.0, 3.5 mm line in/out, HDMI out; dock adds DVD, VGA, three USB 2.0.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): TPM security chip, hardware encryption, drop sensor protecting the hard drive, backlit keyboard.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
Backlit slide-out keyboard; pen.

HOW IT FEELS
Black casing, small enough to fit in a pocket (Wikipedia). Leatherette or aluminium cases were sold.

BLIND USABILITY
These 2004 to 2010 machines ran Windows XP, Vista or 7. Screen readers of that era included JAWS, Window-Eyes and early NVDA (NVDA's first releases date from 2006; current NVDA does not support XP). Narrator in XP and Vista was minimal. No source found documents blind use of these specific UMPCs. Their value today is historical; batteries, drives and drivers are aging. Small hardware keyboards, where present, can be learned by touch.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: very low by modern standards. Suitable only for very old PC games and simple audio games that still support Windows XP or 7 (check each game).

ENTRY: Samsung Q1 family (Q1, Q1 SSD, Q1b, Q1F, Q1 Ultra, Q1 Ultra Premium)
Maker: Samsung Electronics
Released: Q1 in 2006 as one of the first Microsoft Origami UMPCs (NBC News 2006 article; UMPC Wikipedia article); Q1b October 2006. The Samsung Q1 Wikipedia article says "starting in 2007", which is DISPUTED.
Launch price: US$1,099 suggested retail for the Q1 (NBC News, 2006, which called it way too high even for early adopters). SSD version about twice the price of the normal Q1 (Wikipedia).

WHAT IT CAN DO
A 7-inch touchscreen slate with a dual-boot media mode (AVS Now, Windows XP Embedded) that boots straight to a music, photo and video player.

HARDWARE
Processor: Q1: Intel Celeron M ULV 353, 900 MHz. Q1b: VIA C7-M ULV 1 GHz. Q1F: Pentium M 723, 1 GHz. Q1 Ultra: Intel A100/A110 600/800 MHz. Ultra Premium: Core Solo U1500, 1.33 GHz.
Memory: 512 MB DDR2 (Q1, up to 2 GB); 1 GB on Q1F and Ultra.
Graphics: Intel 915GMS chipset graphics (Q1); VIA VX700 (Q1b).
Storage: 40 GB 1.8-inch hard drive (32 GB SSD option); 60 GB options later.
Display: 7-inch 800 by 480 resistive touch (Q1); 1024 by 600 (Ultra).
Battery: 3-cell up to 3 hours or 6-cell up to 6 hours (Q1); 5 hours on Q1b.
Weight and size: 0.78 kg (Q1).
Operating system: Windows XP Tablet PC Edition or Windows Vista Home Premium.
Wi-Fi and network: 802.11b/g.
Bluetooth: Bluetooth (2.0 on Q1b).
Cellular: Optional WiBro or HSDPA module (Q1b); HSDPA on Ultra.
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): Q1: VGA, 100 Mbit LAN, CompactFlash Type II, two USB. Ultra: SD slot replaces CF.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): Q1: stereo speakers, dual array microphones, DMB TV receiver. Q1b: mono speaker, no microphones (external mic jack). Ultra: front camera, dual cameras, joystick usable as a mouse.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
Q1: no hardware keyboard; stylus and the on-screen DialKey thumb keyboard (NBC News called it silly-looking but quite useful). Ultra: split thumb keyboard on either side of the screen plus a joystick.

HOW IT FEELS
A 7-inch slate weighing 0.78 kg, about 1.7 pounds. The Ultra's split keyboard puts half the letters under each thumb on either side of the screen.

BLIND USABILITY
These 2004 to 2010 machines ran Windows XP, Vista or 7. Screen readers of that era included JAWS, Window-Eyes and early NVDA (NVDA's first releases date from 2006; current NVDA does not support XP). Narrator in XP and Vista was minimal. No source found documents blind use of these specific UMPCs. Their value today is historical; batteries, drives and drivers are aging. Small hardware keyboards, where present, can be learned by touch.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: very low by modern standards. Suitable only for very old PC games and simple audio games that still support Windows XP or 7 (check each game).

Release year DISPUTED.

ENTRY: Sony VAIO P series (2009 to 2010)
Maker: Sony
Released: January 2009 (VGN-P500); P600 July 2009; P700 October 2009; VPC-P11 May 2010; discontinued 2011 (Wikipedia).
Launch price: From about US$900 with a 60 GB hard drive up to US$1,499 with a 128 GB SSD (PCWorld, 2009).

WHAT IT CAN DO
A "pocket" clamshell netbook-like computer with an ultra-wide 8-inch screen and a near-full keyboard.

HARDWARE
Processor: Intel Atom Z520 to Z560, 1.33 to 2.13 GHz, single core with Hyper-Threading.
Memory: 1 or 2 GB DDR2-533.
Graphics: Intel GMA 500.
Storage: 60 or 80 GB 4200 rpm hard drive, or 64/128/256 GB SSD.
Display: 8-inch 1600 by 768 LED backlit.
Battery: 2,100 mAh standard, 4,200 mAh extended (launch); 2,500/5,000 mAh (VPC-P11).
Weight and size: 589 to 680 grams; 9.65 by 0.78 by 4.72 inches.
Operating system: Windows Vista at launch (Windows 7 later, UNCERTAIN).
Wi-Fi and network: 802.11b/g/n.
Bluetooth: Bluetooth with A2DP.
Cellular: Region-dependent WiMAX, HSDPA or 3G; GPS on some models.
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): Two USB 2.0, headphone jack, display/LAN adapter port, Memory Stick and SD slots.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): 0.3 MP webcam. VPC-P11 added an accelerometer, a 16 by 16 mm touchpad in the screen bezel, compass, light sensor, and a button that switches the screen to 1280 by 600 to make text larger.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
QWERTY keyboard about 88 percent of desktop size with cut-out keys about half an inch wide, a pointing stick (PCWorld called it the best pointing device yet on such a machine) with firm mouse buttons below the spacebar, and customizable shortcut buttons (PCWorld). VPC-P11 added a bezel touchpad.

HOW IT FEELS
About 9.65 inches wide, 4.72 inches deep and 0.78 inches thick, roughly 1.3 to 1.5 pounds: long and narrow like a business envelope. PCWorld found the keyboard borders on unusably small for touch typing but reasonably spaced.

BLIND USABILITY
These 2004 to 2010 machines ran Windows XP, Vista or 7. Screen readers of that era included JAWS, Window-Eyes and early NVDA (NVDA's first releases date from 2006; current NVDA does not support XP). Narrator in XP and Vista was minimal. No source found documents blind use of these specific UMPCs. Their value today is historical; batteries, drives and drivers are aging. Small hardware keyboards, where present, can be learned by touch.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: very low by modern standards. Suitable only for very old PC games and simple audio games that still support Windows XP or 7 (check each game).

UNCERTAIN
None.

ENTRY: Fujitsu LifeBook U810
Maker: Fujitsu
Released: 2007 (Laptop Mag).
Launch price: US$999 (Laptop Mag, 2007).

WHAT IT CAN DO
The first convertible clamshell UMPC: a tiny laptop whose screen twists to become a tablet (Laptop Mag).

HARDWARE
Processor: Intel A110, 800 MHz (Laptop Mag).
Memory: not documented
Graphics: not documented
Storage: not documented
Display: 5.6-inch Crystal View SVGA touchscreen (Laptop Mag; SVGA is normally 800 by 600, exact panel resolution not stated).
Battery: 5 hours 14 minutes in MobileMark 2007 (rated 5:30).
Weight and size: 1.6 pounds; 6.7 by 6 by 1 inches.
Operating system: Windows Vista (Tablet features) (UNCERTAIN).
Wi-Fi and network: not documented
Bluetooth: not documented
Cellular: No mobile broadband at review time.
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): not documented
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): 0.3 MP webcam and microphone above the screen; fingerprint reader; keyboard lights.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
Pointing stick on the right above the keyboard, two scroll buttons and a Function toggle beside it; mouse buttons and a small speaker on the left. Below the screen: keyboard light button, on-screen keyboard button, screen orientation button. Alt button and fingerprint reader on the right.

HOW IT FEELS
Laptop Mag: small enough for a large purse; a small keyboard with up to three functions per key, and arrow keys that need the Fn key because they share the period and backslash keys. It stayed cool after hours.

BLIND USABILITY
These 2004 to 2010 machines ran Windows XP, Vista or 7. Screen readers of that era included JAWS, Window-Eyes and early NVDA (NVDA's first releases date from 2006; current NVDA does not support XP). Narrator in XP and Vista was minimal. No source found documents blind use of these specific UMPCs. Their value today is historical; batteries, drives and drivers are aging. Small hardware keyboards, where present, can be learned by touch. The U810's arrows-behind-Fn layout would make screen reader navigation awkward.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: very low by modern standards. Suitable only for very old PC games and simple audio games that still support Windows XP or 7 (check each game).

UNCERTAIN
None.

ENTRY: Viliv S5 (2009)
Maker: Yukyung (Viliv brand), South Korea
Released: Mid-2009 (Wikipedia, UMPC article).
Launch price: US$599 (60 GB HDD, no 3G), US$699 (32 GB SSD), US$799 (32 GB SSD plus 3G) (PCWorld).

WHAT IT CAN DO
A 4.8-inch slate UMPC that looks like a car GPS, with GPS and optional 3G; a US$50 car kit made it a navigator.

HARDWARE
Processor: Intel Atom Z520 (UMPC Wikipedia article: Intel Z520).
Memory: not documented
Graphics: Intel GMA 500 with H.264 hardware video decode.
Storage: 60 GB HDD or 32 GB SSD.
Display: 4.8-inch 1024 by 600 touchscreen (PCWorld).
Battery: Viliv: up to 6 hours video, 4.5 hours streaming.
Weight and size: About 14 ounces; 6.0 by 3.3 by 0.9 inches.
Operating system: Windows XP.
Wi-Fi and network: Wi-Fi.
Bluetooth: Bluetooth.
Cellular: Optional 3G (spotty in PCWorld's test).
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): USB port for keyboard.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): On-board speakers that sounded "pretty darn good"; haptic feedback on the software keyboard.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
Four-way joystick; a hardware button on the right of the bezel summons the software keyboard. No physical keyboard.

HOW IT FEELS
PCWorld: about 14 ounces and car-GPS shaped. Fingertip touches don't always land where intended on the tiny screen.

BLIND USABILITY
These 2004 to 2010 machines ran Windows XP, Vista or 7. Screen readers of that era included JAWS, Window-Eyes and early NVDA (NVDA's first releases date from 2006; current NVDA does not support XP). Narrator in XP and Vista was minimal. No source found documents blind use of these specific UMPCs. Their value today is historical; batteries, drives and drivers are aging. Small hardware keyboards, where present, can be learned by touch. No physical keyboard; a USB keyboard would be essential.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: very low by modern standards. Suitable only for very old PC games and simple audio games that still support Windows XP or 7 (check each game).

None.
