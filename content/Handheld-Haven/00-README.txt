HANDHELD HAVEN
Your one-stop shop for handheld device information.

An accessible plain text encyclopedia of handheld and small Windows computers, from 2004 pocket PCs and the original GPD Win to the ROG Xbox Ally X, written for blind and low vision buyers.

WHAT THIS IS
Handheld Haven is a collection of plain UTF-8 text files. There are no tables, images or special formatting. Every heading is in capital letters on its own line, so you can find it with your screen reader's find command or by reading line by line. Every device entry starts with a line that begins with the word ENTRY and a colon, so searching for "ENTRY:" jumps from device to device.

WHY IT EXISTS
Some handheld PCs cost less than a laptop, and some have a keyboard built in. For a blind person, the questions that matter are rarely in spec sheets. Can I turn on a screen reader without sighted help? Can I feel which button is which? Will my audio games run? Handheld Haven collects what is publicly documented, says plainly when something is not documented, and tries to help you decide whether a handheld is worth buying.

HOW TO READ IT
Start with 01-INDEX.txt for the list of files and devices. Read 03-blind-buyers-guide.txt if you are deciding what to buy. Read 04-screen-reader-setup.txt before setting up a device. Each numbered family file covers one maker or product line. Handheld-Haven-complete.txt contains everything in one file, in order, for people who prefer a single document.

EACH DEVICE ENTRY HAS THESE LABELED LINES
The entry line, which starts with ENTRY and a colon, then the device name.
Maker. Released. Launch price (with currency, source and year where found).
What it can do: a plain overview of its capabilities.
A hardware section: processor, memory, graphics, storage, display, battery, weight and size, operating system, Wi-Fi and network, Bluetooth, cellular, ports and expansion, other hardware.
Controls and button layout.
How it feels: a physical, tactile description drawn from reviews.
Blind usability: Narrator, NVDA and JAWS, how to start a screen reader, controller navigation, audio, setup and maker software.
Game compatibility: performance tier, known issues, anti-cheat and audio games.
Uncertain: anything disputed, unclear, or drawn from weaker sources.
Sources.

HONESTY NOTES
"not documented" means no source used for this book stated it. It does not mean the feature is missing. UNCERTAIN means a source was weak or unclear. DISPUTED means sources disagree, and both values are given. THIN ENTRY marks devices with little reliable information. Prices are launch prices from the dates shown, not current prices; prices change, and some later rose. Nobody involved in writing this book tested these devices with a screen reader. Blind usability notes come from published reviews, forum reports from blind users, and Microsoft and maker documentation, and are credited where used.

HOW IT WAS MADE
Handheld Haven was compiled with AI assistance from public sources, then structured for screen readers. See 07-build-notes.txt for the method and the known limits, and 06-sources.txt for every web address used. Check anything important, especially prices and compatibility, before you buy.
