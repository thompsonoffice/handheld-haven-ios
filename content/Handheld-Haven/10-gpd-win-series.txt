GPD WIN SERIES

GPD (Shenzhen GPD Technology) began the modern Windows gaming handheld with the crowdfunded GPD Win in 2016. The Win line mixes thumb keyboards and game controls.

ENTRY: GPD Win (original)
Also known as: GPD Win 1
Maker: GamePad Digital (GPD), Shenzhen, China
Released: Announced October 2015; crowdfunded on Indiegogo in 2016; shipped from October 2016 (Wikipedia).
Launch price: Indiegogo pre-order backers were offered US$330 (Wikipedia) and Liliputing reported a US$299 early crowdfunding price; planned retail was US$499 (Laptop Mag via search pointer, UNCERTAIN); Liliputing's October 2016 review noted US$340 from GPD's AliExpress store.

WHAT IT CAN DO
A full x86 Windows 10 computer the size of a large Nintendo DS. It runs ordinary Windows desktop programs, older and lighter PC games, emulators and audio games, and it has a QWERTY keyboard built in. It is not powerful enough for modern 3D games.

HARDWARE
Processor: Intel Atom x7-Z8700 (first units shipped with x7-Z8750, then GPD standardized on the Z8700 because it performed better, per Liliputing). Quad-core, four threads. The Z8700 is the same chip Microsoft used in the Surface 3, whose Wikipedia infobox lists 1.6 GHz base and up to 2.4 GHz, 2 W scenario design power.
Memory: 4 GB LPDDR3, soldered (not upgradeable).
Graphics: Intel HD Graphics 405 (integrated). Compute units: not documented.
Storage: 64 GB eMMC 4.51 (soldered) plus one microSD slot.
Display: 5.5-inch 1280 by 720 capacitive multi-touch, Gorilla Glass 3.
Battery: 6,900 mAh (Wikipedia) or 6,700 mAh (Liliputing), DISPUTED; GPD claimed 6 to 8 hours.
Weight and size: About 13 ounces (Liliputing); 155 by 97 by 22 millimetres (6.1 by 3.8 by 0.9 inches).
Operating system: Windows 10 Home.
Wi-Fi and network: Wi-Fi 802.11a/b/g/n/ac, dual band 2.4 and 5 GHz. Wi-Fi chip: not documented.
Bluetooth: Bluetooth (version not documented in sources used).
Cellular: None.
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): One USB 3.0 Type-A, one USB-C (charging), mini HDMI, headset jack, microSD. No Thunderbolt or USB4; no eGPU support documented.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): Fingerprint reader: none documented. Cameras: none documented. One speaker on the side. Haptics and gyro: not documented. Mouse-mode switch between the sticks.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
D-pad, four face buttons (X, Y, A, B), two analog sticks, L1, L2, R1, R2 shoulder buttons, L3 and R3 as small keys to the right of the keyboard, plus a full QWERTY keyboard with an Fn key. A slide switch between the sticks turns on mouse mode: right stick moves the pointer, left stick scrolls, shoulder buttons click.

HOW IT FEELS
Picture a thick clamshell, a little smaller than a paperback, about 6 inches wide and almost an inch thick, weighing about as much as a full can of soup. You lift the lid like a Nintendo DS. The lower half has the D-pad and left stick on the upper left, the face buttons and right stick on the upper right, and a tiny chiclet keyboard filling the space below them. Liliputing describes a glossy black plastic case and says the gaming buttons "feel pretty good". The keyboard is too small for touch typing; it is meant for passwords, URLs and short commands. The single speaker sits on the side where your palm naturally rests, so it is easy to muffle it with your hand.

BLIND USABILITY
GPD's own software and tools: accessibility with a screen reader is not documented. Windows itself includes Narrator (start or stop with Windows key+Ctrl+Enter on the built-in or an external keyboard). Because GPD Win models from the Win, Win 2, Win Max, Win 3 and Win 4 have a built-in physical or touch keyboard, you can usually press Windows+Ctrl+Enter without plugging anything in, which is a real advantage over keyboard-less handhelds. NVDA or JAWS can be installed like on any Windows PC. Out-of-box Windows setup with Narrator on GPD hardware has not been tested in any source found; plan on a USB or Bluetooth keyboard and, if possible, a sighted helper for the first boot. The built-in keyboard means NVDA keystrokes (Insert or Caps Lock combinations) are physically available, but the tiny keys and Fn layer make them hard to find by touch; Liliputing notes Fn keys are triggered by holding Fn and pressing the number row.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: very low (Atom era). Liliputing and Windows Central reported it as suitable for older and less demanding PC games and emulation; Windows Central put it at roughly Xbox 360-era and lighter titles. Text and audio games, NVDA-friendly indie titles and most AudioGames.net-style games ask very little of the CPU and are a good fit. Anti-cheat: no specific issues documented. Expect Windows 10 to be the last practical version; Windows 11 is not officially supported on this Atom chip (Microsoft's processor list is not cited here, treat as UNCERTAIN).

UNCERTAIN
Battery capacity is DISPUTED (6,900 vs 6,700 mAh). The US$499 planned retail figure comes from a search summary of Laptop Mag and Liliputing and is UNCERTAIN.

ENTRY: GPD Win 2
Maker: GamePad Digital (GPD)
Released: Indiegogo campaign early 2018; shipped May 2018 (Wikipedia).
Launch price: US$649 Indiegogo pre-order (Liliputing, 2018). Retail price: not documented in sources used.

WHAT IT CAN DO
A pocket Windows 10 PC with the same class of Core m3 chip as entry-level Surface Pro and Surface Laptop models (Liliputing). Plays older and mid-weight PC games, emulators, and light office work; can drive an external display, keyboard and mouse.

HARDWARE
Processor: Intel Core m3 (Wikipedia lists Core m3 with HD 615 graphics; the exact model, commonly reported as m3-7Y30 then m3-8100Y, is UNCERTAIN). Dual-core, four threads; clocks and TDP not documented in sources used.
Memory: 8 GB (type: not documented in sources used; soldered).
Graphics: Intel HD Graphics 615 (integrated).
Storage: 128 GB M.2 SSD, replaceable (Wikipedia), plus microSD.
Display: 6-inch 1280 by 720, 10-point touch.
Battery: not documented
Weight and size: 162 by 99 by 25 millimetres (6.4 by 3.9 by 1 inch). Weight: not documented in sources used.
Operating system: Windows 10 Home.
Wi-Fi and network: Wi-Fi 802.11a/b/g/n/ac dual band; chip not documented.
Bluetooth: Bluetooth (version not documented).
Cellular: None.
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): USB 3.0 Type-A, USB-C, micro HDMI, headset jack, microSD. No Thunderbolt; eGPU not documented.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): Fingerprint, cameras, haptics, gyro: not documented.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
D-pad, four face buttons, six shoulder buttons, two analog sticks, a small clamshell keyboard with tactile keys and bumps on F and J (Liliputing). Joystick mouse mode with shoulder buttons as clicks.

HOW IT FEELS
A slightly bigger, thicker clamshell than the original Win, about 6.4 inches wide and an inch thick. Open the lid and the controls sit above a thumb keyboard. Liliputing says GPD made the keyboard tactile with little bumps on the J and F keys, but the keys are much smaller than a fingertip, so typing more than a few words "feels like a chore".

BLIND USABILITY
GPD's own software and tools: accessibility with a screen reader is not documented. Windows itself includes Narrator (start or stop with Windows key+Ctrl+Enter on the built-in or an external keyboard). Because GPD Win models from the Win, Win 2, Win Max, Win 3 and Win 4 have a built-in physical or touch keyboard, you can usually press Windows+Ctrl+Enter without plugging anything in, which is a real advantage over keyboard-less handhelds. NVDA or JAWS can be installed like on any Windows PC. Out-of-box Windows setup with Narrator on GPD hardware has not been tested in any source found; plan on a USB or Bluetooth keyboard and, if possible, a sighted helper for the first boot. The F and J bumps are a genuine help for finding your place on the keyboard by touch.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: low to low-mid. Liliputing found Rayman Origins, Psychonauts and Deadlight played smoothly with the built-in controller, and played StarCraft II on an external display. Good for audio games, older titles and emulation up to roughly the sixth console generation (UNCERTAIN, general expectation, not tested in sources).

UNCERTAIN
Exact Core m3 model and battery size not confirmed in sources used.

ENTRY: GPD Win Max
Maker: GamePad Digital (GPD)
Released: Indiegogo campaign launched May 18, 2020, ended July 1, 2020; shipped from August 2020 (Wikipedia).
Launch price: US$779 on Indiegogo, later raised to US$800 (Wikipedia). More than 3,500 backers contributed over US$2.8 million.

WHAT IT CAN DO
A tiny gaming laptop: an 8-inch clamshell with a real laptop-style keyboard and touchpad plus game controls above the keyboard. Runs mid-weight PC games of its era and full desktop work.

HARDWARE
Processor: Intel Core i5-1035G7 (Ice Lake, four cores, eight threads; clocks and TDP setting not documented in sources used).
Memory: 16 GB DDR4 (soldered status not documented).
Graphics: Intel Iris Plus Graphics 940 (integrated, per Wikipedia).
Storage: 512 GB SSD, replaceable, plus microSD.
Display: 8-inch 1280 by 800 (16:10), 10-point touch.
Battery: Three 5,000 mAh lithium-ion cells in series; USB Power Delivery 3.0, 65 W charging. Some users reported failure of the included USB-C power supply (Wikipedia).
Weight and size: 790 grams (1.7 pounds); 207 by 145 by 26 millimetres.
Operating system: Windows 10 Home.
Wi-Fi and network: Wi-Fi 6 (chip not documented).
Bluetooth: Bluetooth 5.0.
Cellular: None.
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): Two USB-A 5 Gbps, one USB-C 10 Gbps, one Thunderbolt 3, HDMI, 3.5 mm headset jack, microSD. Thunderbolt 3 makes an external GPU enclosure possible in principle (not tested in sources).
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): Touchpad (3-point, one button). Fingerprint, camera, haptics, gyro: not documented.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
XInput controller, D-pad, four face buttons, four shoulder buttons, two clickable analog sticks, placed above a laptop keyboard.

HOW IT FEELS
Think of a very small, thick laptop, about 8 inches wide, 5.7 inches deep and an inch thick, weighing about as much as a hardcover book. When you open it, your thumbs find the D-pad and sticks along the top edge of the keyboard deck, above the letter keys.

BLIND USABILITY
GPD's own software and tools: accessibility with a screen reader is not documented. Windows itself includes Narrator (start or stop with Windows key+Ctrl+Enter on the built-in or an external keyboard). Because GPD Win models from the Win, Win 2, Win Max, Win 3 and Win 4 have a built-in physical or touch keyboard, you can usually press Windows+Ctrl+Enter without plugging anything in, which is a real advantage over keyboard-less handhelds. NVDA or JAWS can be installed like on any Windows PC. Out-of-box Windows setup with Narrator on GPD hardware has not been tested in any source found; plan on a USB or Bluetooth keyboard and, if possible, a sighted helper for the first boot. Its laptop-style keyboard is the most usable for screen reader commands of the early Win models.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: low-mid for 2020. Fine for audio games, older 3D titles and lighter indie games.

UNCERTAIN
Whether the RAM is soldered is not documented.

ENTRY: GPD Win 3
Maker: GamePad Digital (GPD)
Released: Announced January 2021; Indiegogo January to March 2021 (Wikipedia).
Launch price: US$799 on Indiegogo (Notebookcheck).

WHAT IT CAN DO
A slider handheld: the screen slides up to reveal a backlit touch keyboard. A much bigger performance jump than earlier Wins thanks to Intel Tiger Lake, with Thunderbolt 4 for docking.

HARDWARE
Processor: Intel Core i5-1135G7 or Core i7-1165G7; later i7-1195G7 (Wikipedia). Four cores, eight threads (Tiger Lake). TDP settings: not documented in sources used.
Memory: 16 GB LPDDR4x-4266, soldered.
Graphics: Intel Iris Xe integrated (80 EU on the i5, 96 EU on the i7, per Notebookcheck).
Storage: SSD, replaceable, plus spring-loaded microSD (Notebookcheck).
Display: 5.5-inch 1280 by 720, 10-point touch, under a thick layer of Gorilla Glass (Notebookcheck).
Battery: 3 by 5,000 mAh, 57 Wh (Wikipedia, as listed). Notebookcheck: just over 11 hours of Wi-Fi browsing, no more than 1.5 hours of 3D gaming; about 1.5 hours to fully charge with the 65 W adapter.
Weight and size: 560 grams (Wikipedia) or 553 grams (Notebookcheck); 198 by 92 by 27 millimetres.
Operating system: Windows 10 Home, later Windows 11 Home.
Wi-Fi and network: Wi-Fi 6 (some units shipped with the wrong wireless adapter, per Liliputing's GPD Duo review). Chip: not documented.
Bluetooth: Bluetooth 5.0.
Cellular: None.
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): USB-A 5 Gbps, USB-C with Thunderbolt 4, HDMI (via dock per some reports; Wikipedia lists HDMI), 3.5 mm headset jack, microSD. Thunderbolt 4 allows eGPU docks in principle.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): Touch-sensitive backlit keyboard with no physical keys (Notebookcheck). Touchpad listed in infobox (3-point). No webcam (Notebookcheck wished for one). Haptics and gyro: not documented.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
D-pad, four face buttons, shoulder buttons and triggers, two sticks around a sliding 5.5-inch screen; a flat capacitive keyboard underneath. The integrated controller shows up as an Xbox controller in Steam games (Notebookcheck).

HOW IT FEELS
A smooth, rounded handheld about 7.8 inches wide and just over an inch thick, about the weight of a large smartphone and a half. The screen slides up like an old BlackBerry slider. Notebookcheck found the controller arrow keys and shoulder buttons "weak and shallow in feedback" compared with a DualShock or Switch, reported occasional stuck buttons, and said sliding the screen up often registers stray key presses. The keyboard is glass-flat with no key edges, so it gives you nothing to feel.

BLIND USABILITY
GPD's own software and tools: accessibility with a screen reader is not documented. Windows itself includes Narrator (start or stop with Windows key+Ctrl+Enter on the built-in or an external keyboard). Because GPD Win models from the Win, Win 2, Win Max, Win 3 and Win 4 have a built-in physical or touch keyboard, you can usually press Windows+Ctrl+Enter without plugging anything in, which is a real advantage over keyboard-less handhelds. NVDA or JAWS can be installed like on any Windows PC. Out-of-box Windows setup with Narrator on GPD hardware has not been tested in any source found; plan on a USB or Bluetooth keyboard and, if possible, a sighted helper for the first boot. Important caution for the Win 3: its keyboard is a flat touch panel with no physical keys (Notebookcheck), so finding Windows+Ctrl+Enter or NVDA keys by touch is close to impossible. Plan on an external keyboard.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: mid for 2021. Notebookcheck tested The Witcher 3 under stress. Good for audio games, indie and many older AAA games at low settings.

UNCERTAIN
HDMI on the unit versus dock is listed inconsistently; treat as UNCERTAIN.

ENTRY: GPD Win 4 (including 2023, 2024 and 2025 refreshes)
Maker: GamePad Digital (GPD)
Released: Indiegogo campaign started December 2022; first orders fulfilled March 2023 (Wikipedia). Later refreshes added newer AMD chips.
Launch price: US$799 (16 GB/512 GB) to US$1,199 (32 GB/2 TB) on Indiegogo; 4G module US$100 extra (Notebookcheck, 2022).

WHAT IT CAN DO
A PlayStation Vita-like slider with a real slide-out physical keyboard and AMD graphics strong enough for many modern games at low settings. Also offered with SteamOS per Wikipedia.

HARDWARE
Processor: AMD Ryzen 7 6800U at launch; later Ryzen 5 7640U, Ryzen 7 7840U, Ryzen 5 8640U, Ryzen 7 8840U, and Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 versions (Wikipedia). The 6800U is eight cores, sixteen threads (Zen 3+). TDP settings: not documented in sources used.
Memory: 16 to 32 GB LPDDR5x-6400, or 16 to 64 GB LPDDR5x-7500 on newer versions (Wikipedia). Soldered.
Graphics: AMD Radeon 680M, 760M, 780M or 890M depending on chip (integrated).
Storage: 512 GB to 4 TB SSD, replaceable, plus microSD.
Display: 6-inch 1920 by 1080, 10-point touch.
Battery: 45.62 Wh (11,849 mAh).
Weight and size: 598 grams (1.3 pounds); 220 by 92 by 28 millimetres.
Operating system: Windows 11 or SteamOS (Wikipedia).
Wi-Fi and network: Wi-Fi 6 (chip not documented).
Bluetooth: Bluetooth 5.2.
Cellular: Optional 4G module (US$100, Notebookcheck).
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): USB-C 40 Gbps (USB4), USB-C 10 Gbps, HDMI through dock, 3.5 mm headset jack, microSD. USB4 allows eGPU docks in principle.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): Touchpad (3-point, one button). Fingerprint, cameras, haptics, gyro: not documented in sources used.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
D-pad, face buttons, two sticks, shoulder buttons and triggers around a 6-inch screen that slides up to expose a physical backlit QWERTY keyboard (Liliputing's GPD Duo review describes the Win 4 as having a slide-out keyboard).

HOW IT FEELS
About 8.7 inches wide, 3.6 inches tall and just over an inch thick, weighing about 1.3 pounds, roughly the weight of two large phones. The grips curve back like a PS Vita. Slide the screen up and a small physical keyboard appears.

BLIND USABILITY
GPD's own software and tools: accessibility with a screen reader is not documented. Windows itself includes Narrator (start or stop with Windows key+Ctrl+Enter on the built-in or an external keyboard). Because GPD Win models from the Win, Win 2, Win Max, Win 3 and Win 4 have a built-in physical or touch keyboard, you can usually press Windows+Ctrl+Enter without plugging anything in, which is a real advantage over keyboard-less handhelds. NVDA or JAWS can be installed like on any Windows PC. Out-of-box Windows setup with Narrator on GPD hardware has not been tested in any source found; plan on a USB or Bluetooth keyboard and, if possible, a sighted helper for the first boot. The slide-out keyboard is physical (unlike the Win 3's touch panel), so Windows+Ctrl+Enter is reachable on the device itself.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: mid (6800U) to upper-mid (8840U, HX 370). Plays many current games at reduced settings, all audio games, and emulation. Anti-cheat: Windows-native, so games with kernel anti-cheat generally install as on a PC; specific issues not documented.

UNCERTAIN
Wikipedia's infobox gives a 2021 release date, which conflicts with its own text (campaign December 2022); this book uses the text.

ENTRY: GPD Win Max 2
Maker: GamePad Digital (GPD)
Released: Indiegogo mid-2022; estimated shipping September 2022 (Windows Central). Release listed as October 2022 by Lost In Consoles.
Launch price: US$899 limited edition (50 units, 6800U/16 GB/128 GB), US$999 standard, up to US$1,299 (Windows Central, July 2022). Notebookcheck: about US$1,000 for either chip.

WHAT IT CAN DO
A 10.1-inch mini gaming laptop with a full keyboard, touchpad and game controls. Doubles as a capable small office laptop with LTE.

HARDWARE
Processor: AMD Ryzen 7 6800U (8 cores, 16 threads) or Intel Core i7-1260P (12 cores, 16 threads; Notebookcheck measured 28 W sustained, 35 W short burst). Later refreshes exist with newer AMD chips (not detailed here).
Memory: 16 or 32 GB LPDDR5, soldered (Notebookcheck).
Graphics: AMD Radeon 680M or Intel Iris Xe 96 EU (integrated).
Storage: 1 TB or 2 TB SSD (Notebookcheck test unit: BiWin 1 TB M.2). Full-size SD and microSD readers.
Display: 10.1-inch 2560 by 1600 (16:10), 60 Hz, glossy IPS touchscreen with pen support.
Battery: 70 Wh (Notebookcheck).
Weight and size: About 1.005 to 1.042 kg (2.2 to 2.3 pounds); 227 by 160 by 23 millimetres.
Operating system: Windows 11 Home.
Wi-Fi and network: Intel Wi-Fi 6E AX210 (Notebookcheck's Intel unit).
Bluetooth: Bluetooth 5.2.
Cellular: Quectel LTE EG25-G modem in Notebookcheck's unit.
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): Notebookcheck lists Thunderbolt (Intel unit), USB-C with Power Delivery, USB-A, HDMI, 3.5 mm combo jack, SD and microSD. eGPU via Thunderbolt possible in principle.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): Fingerprint reader, brightness sensor, accelerometer, webcam, quad speakers, backlit chiclet keyboard (Notebookcheck). Haptics and gyro: not documented.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
Game controls (sticks, D-pad, face buttons) above the keyboard, and shoulder buttons along the back edge; magnetic covers hide the sticks when typing (widely reported, UNCERTAIN as not in sources used).

HOW IT FEELS
A small, solid magnesium-aluminium alloy laptop (Lost In Consoles), about 9 by 6.3 inches and under an inch thick, weighing about as much as a large hardcover dictionary. Its keyboard is close to netbook size and is the most typing-friendly of any Win model.

BLIND USABILITY
GPD's own software and tools: accessibility with a screen reader is not documented. Windows itself includes Narrator (start or stop with Windows key+Ctrl+Enter on the built-in or an external keyboard). Because GPD Win models from the Win, Win 2, Win Max, Win 3 and Win 4 have a built-in physical or touch keyboard, you can usually press Windows+Ctrl+Enter without plugging anything in, which is a real advantage over keyboard-less handhelds. NVDA or JAWS can be installed like on any Windows PC. Out-of-box Windows setup with Narrator on GPD hardware has not been tested in any source found; plan on a USB or Bluetooth keyboard and, if possible, a sighted helper for the first boot. Of all GPD Win models this is the most screen-reader-friendly by layout, because the keyboard is near laptop size and backlit.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: mid for 2022. Good for most indie and older AAA games, all audio games, and emulation.

UNCERTAIN
Magnetic stick covers not confirmed in fetched sources.
