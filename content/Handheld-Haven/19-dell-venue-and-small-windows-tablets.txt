ENTRY: Dell Venue 8 Pro (2013)
Maker: Dell
Released: Announced October 2, 2013 with the Venue 11 Pro (Wikipedia); reviewed November 2013 (The Verge).
Launch price: US$299.99 (The Verge, 2013).

WHAT IT CAN DO
An 8-inch tablet running full Windows 8.1, able to run desktop programs and Office; with a monitor, keyboard and mouse, a tiny desktop.

HARDWARE
Processor: Intel Atom Z3740D, quad-core, 1.8 GHz burst (The Verge).
Memory: 2 GB (The Verge).
Graphics: Intel HD (Bay Trail).
Storage: 32 or 64 GB; microSD under a flap.
Display: 8-inch IPS, 1280 by 800 (The Verge). The Verge found a broken auto-brightness at launch.
Battery: The Verge Battery Test: 11 hours 55 minutes; 7 hours 33 minutes with auto-brightness off.
Weight and size: 13.9 ounces (The Verge); 13.6 ounces (Laptop Mag); 9 mm thick.
Operating system: Windows 8.1 with Office 2013.
Wi-Fi and network: not documented
Bluetooth: not documented
Cellular: not documented
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): One micro-USB (also for charging), headphone jack, microSD.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): One loudspeaker on the bottom. Windows key on the top edge that doubles as part of the power button.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
Power button and volume rocker on the right side; Windows key on top next to the headphone jack; left side clear.

HOW IT FEELS
The Verge: comfortable to hold in one hand with weight well distributed; a black plastic slab with a textured, rubber-like back with grooves that grips your fingers, matte so it resists fingerprints.

BLIND USABILITY
These 8-inch Windows 8.1 tablets have a hardware Windows button, so Microsoft's touch-device shortcut, Windows button+Volume Up, is the documented way to start Narrator without a keyboard (whether it works on Windows 8.1 the same way as on Windows 10 and 11 is UNCERTAIN). Narrator in Windows 8.1 supported touch gestures, but Narrator has improved greatly since; these tablets are now old, slow and limited to 32-bit Windows in many cases (UNCERTAIN per model), so NVDA versions that require 64-bit Windows may not install. Treat them as collectibles or very light text devices. The Verge notes the Windows key is on the top edge, not below the screen, so feel along the top beside the headphone jack.

GAME COMPATIBILITY
Performance tier: very low by modern standards. Suitable only for very old PC games and simple audio games that still support Windows XP or 7 (check each game).

UNCERTAIN
None.

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell_Venue

ENTRY: Dell Venue 11 Pro (2013) and Venue 11 Pro 7000
Maker: Dell
Released: Venue 11 Pro announced October 2013 (Wikipedia); 7000 series reviewed by Laptop Mag around 2015.
Launch price: Venue 11 Pro 7000 starts at US$699; Laptop Mag's reviewed configuration was US$1,259 with stylus and keyboard. Original 2013 price not documented in sources used.

A 10.8-inch Windows business tablet with a battery-equipped keyboard dock and pen.

Memory: not documented
Graphics: not documented
Storage: not documented
Display: 10.8-inch (Laptop Mag).
Battery: 38 Wh; Laptop Mag: 8 hours alone, 13 hours 33 minutes with the keyboard dock.
Weight and size: 1.6 pounds tablet; 3.6 pounds with keyboard dock; 11.01 by 6.95 by 0.42 inches.
Operating system: Windows 8.1.
Wi-Fi and network: not documented
Bluetooth: not documented
Cellular: not documented
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): Left edge: headphone jack, volume buttons, speaker, micro-USB power, micro HDMI, USB 3.0. Right edge: power, microSD, speaker, lock slot (Laptop Mag). Optional US$80 Dell Tablet Dock adds three USB 3.0, DisplayPort, HDMI, audio and Ethernet.
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): Waves MaxxAudio speakers measured 86 dB, louder than the Surface Pro 3; 8 MP camera; active stylus.

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
No game controls.

Laptop Mag: a matte black back with a soft-touch finish. The keyboard dock has 1.3 mm key travel but needs 71 grams of force per key, stiffer than the Surface Type Cover; no backlight.

Narrator in Windows 8.1 supported touch gestures, but Narrator has improved greatly since; these tablets are now old, slow and limited to 32-bit Windows in many cases (UNCERTAIN per model), so NVDA versions that require 64-bit Windows may not install. Treat them as collectibles or very light text devices. The battery keyboard dock gives a real laptop keyboard for screen reader commands.

GAME COMPATIBILITY

UNCERTAIN

ENTRY: HP Stream 7 (2014)

Launch price: US$99, with a year of Office 365 Personal and a US$25 Windows Store gift card (Thurrott).

One of the cheapest full Windows tablets ever sold; a 7-inch Windows 8.1 tablet for basic home use.

HARDWARE
Processor: Intel Atom Z3735G, quad-core 1.8 GHz.
Memory: 1 GB.
Graphics: Intel HD (Bay Trail).
Storage: 32 GB eMMC; microSD (UNCERTAIN).
Display: 7-inch 1280 by 800 IPS.
Battery: HP rated up to 8 hours; under 5 hours in Thurrott's video test; non-removable.
Weight and size: Not given; Thurrott calls it chunky, heavy and thick.
Operating system: Windows 8.1 with Bing (UNCERTAIN edition name).
Wi-Fi and network: not documented
Bluetooth: not documented
Cellular: not documented
Ports and expansion (USB4, Thunderbolt, eGPU, video out, microSD, docking): not documented
Other hardware (fingerprint or Windows Hello, cameras, speakers, headphone jack, haptics, gyro): Single mono speaker that is loud and clear (Thurrott).

CONTROLS AND BUTTON LAYOUT
Hardware Windows button (UNCERTAIN location).

HOW IT FEELS
Thurrott: durable-feeling but chunky, heavy and thick; a removable back cover that is very hard to remove.

BLIND USABILITY
These 8-inch Windows 8.1 tablets have a hardware Windows button, so Microsoft's touch-device shortcut, Windows button+Volume Up, is the documented way to start Narrator without a keyboard (whether it works on Windows 8.1 the same way as on Windows 10 and 11 is UNCERTAIN). Narrator in Windows 8.1 supported touch gestures, but Narrator has improved greatly since; these tablets are now old, slow and limited to 32-bit Windows in many cases (UNCERTAIN per model), so NVDA versions that require 64-bit Windows may not install. Treat them as collectibles or very light text devices. With only 1 GB of RAM and 32 GB storage, running a modern screen reader will be very slow.

Performance tier: very low by modern standards. Suitable only for very old PC games and simple audio games that still support Windows XP or 7 (check each game).
